From Growth to Depth

India can build quickly. Version 1.0.1 asks the more difficult question: can the country make each new road, machine, factory, chip programme and small firm strengthen the rest of the economy? The economic tree is no longer a sequence of construction bonuses. It is a fight over integration, supplier density, productivity and whether spectacular investment becomes ordinary competence.

Economic Direction · 35 Days

The Development Ledger

Economic growth is indispensable, but a headline rate tells the player only that the economy became larger. It does not say whether a worker reached a better job, whether a truck moved faster, whether a school produced stronger learning, whether firms climbed into more sophisticated production, or whether public investment reduced the cost of doing business. The Development Ledger therefore changes the campaign’s economic vocabulary from “how much was spent?” to “what capacity did the spending create?”

The ledger tracks a deliberately mixed set of outcomes: freight time, grid reliability, industrial value added, labour productivity, formal job creation, learning, housing access near employment and the share of firms able to export or supply larger manufacturers. No single measure becomes sacred. India is too diverse for one number to describe transformation, and a government that optimizes one metric can easily damage another. The point is disciplined attention rather than numerical fetishism.

Once activated, the ledger makes the economic branch harder because projects compete on expected system effects. A politically attractive megaproject can still be chosen, but the game will show whether it connects to ports, industrial clusters, housing and skills. The player gains fewer instant rewards and more compounding bonuses later. Development becomes less cinematic precisely because the gains begin to appear in places a ribbon-cutting ceremony cannot capture.

Public Investment · 42 Days

The Public Capex Machine

The FY2026–27 Union Budget proposes roughly ₹12.2 lakh crore in public capital expenditure, continuing a decade in which infrastructure investment has become one of the central instruments of Indian economic policy. At this scale the question is no longer whether the state can announce projects. It is whether appraisal, land, finance, contracting, engineering and maintenance can absorb investment without turning speed into cost escalation.

The focus creates a national pipeline rather than a pile of schemes. Mature projects move first; projects with unresolved land or utility conflicts are forced to solve them before construction; large programmes publish lifecycle maintenance costs alongside construction costs. States gain incentives to coordinate land and local approvals early, while the Union gains fewer opportunities to promise a start date before the project is actually ready to start.

The political tradeoff is uncomfortable. Better sequencing can make a government look slower in the first year because fewer half-ready projects are ceremonially launched. Over time, however, completion rates rise, contractors face less idle time and private investors begin to trust that connected infrastructure will actually arrive. The machine becomes powerful when the state can repeat delivery without needing every project to become a national emergency.

Freight & Market Integration · 42 Days

Logistics Is a Market

A factory does not compete only on the factory floor. It competes through the road to the warehouse, the rail terminal, the port gate, customs, inventory financing, truck turnaround time and the reliability of every handoff between them. India’s logistics problem is therefore not one infrastructure deficit but a market composed of physical networks and rules. Building a faster railway cannot deliver its full value if the container waits outside the terminal.

The focus integrates freight rail, highways, ports, warehousing, digital documentation and state border processes into corridor-level performance targets. A shipment is measured from factory gate to destination rather than by the speed of one segment. This exposes bottlenecks that individual ministries might otherwise declare outside their responsibility. It also makes boring reforms—standardized documents, appointment systems, better loading yards—mechanically valuable.

As reliability rises, firms can carry less inventory and locate farther from final markets without accepting prohibitive uncertainty. That matters for smaller manufacturers most of all, because large firms can purchase resilience through private warehouses and dedicated logistics. A more integrated logistics market effectively socializes reliability across the economy, allowing industrial geography to broaden beyond a handful of advantaged clusters.

Network Infrastructure · 56 Days

The Corridor Republic

Infrastructure becomes transformative when separate projects behave like a network. A freight corridor without industrial land, a high-speed line without urban transit, a port without hinterland rail or a highway without logistics facilities can move impressive numbers while leaving productivity gains stranded. The Corridor Republic is an attempt to plan connective tissue rather than celebrate isolated assets.

Each major corridor receives an economic geography plan: where housing must grow, where water and electricity are constrained, where industrial clusters can expand, where feeder rail or bus systems are necessary, and where land values will rise enough to displace the workers the corridor is supposed to benefit. The focus therefore forces transport planning to speak to urban planning and industrial policy before construction rather than afterward.

The payoff is compounding. A corridor that reduces freight time, opens new labour markets and connects firms to ports increases the return on private investment across multiple states. The player will notice fewer giant one-time bonuses and more small modifiers appearing in manufacturing, exports, urban development and regional growth. The map begins to function as an economy rather than a collection of lines.

Industrial Structure · 49 Days

Manufacturing Depth

Assembly creates jobs and scale, but industrial power deepens when firms can source more of the difficult inputs around the assembly line: precision parts, specialty materials, controls, testing equipment, industrial software, dies, moulds, tooling and engineering services. A country that imports the machinery and components for every new factory can grow rapidly while remaining vulnerable at the points of highest technical complexity.

This focus makes domestic value chains a ladder rather than a slogan. Incentives gradually shift toward suppliers that improve quality, certify processes and sell to multiple customers instead of surviving on one protected contract. Large manufacturers receive rewards for developing local suppliers, but local content is not treated as a virtue when it simply raises cost and lowers quality indefinitely. The objective is learning that survives competition.

The branch becomes more demanding because industrial depth requires time. A precision supplier may need years of process improvement before it can enter a global chain. The game therefore rewards persistence, engineering education and procurement that values lifecycle performance. Manufacturing stops being measured by the number of factory roofs and begins to be measured by how much knowledge sits inside them.

Semiconductors · 56 Days

Semicon 2.0

In July 2026 the Union Cabinet approved Semicon 2.0 with a total outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore and six pillars spanning design, equipment and materials, fabs, advanced packaging, research and talent. That architecture matters because a semiconductor industry cannot be reduced to the glamour of a fabrication plant. It depends on chemicals, gases, tools, clean-room construction, metrology, packaging, design software, IP, technicians and a culture of process control that is unforgiving of improvisation.

The focus therefore distributes progress across an ecosystem. Design strength earns little if it never produces reusable domestic IP; a fab earns less if almost every machine and material remains external; packaging matters because it is both a large market and a route toward capability; universities matter because talent shortages can convert capital investment into idle equipment. The player must balance visible capacity with the less visible supplier base that makes capacity durable.

The strategic reward is not autarky. No serious semiconductor ecosystem is fully national. The reward is bargaining power created by competence: India can participate in global supply chains from more positions, absorb technology more effectively and withstand disruptions with more options. Semicon 2.0 becomes one of the clearest examples in the tree of “self-reliance” meaning deeper interdependence on stronger terms rather than economic isolation.

Machine Tools & Equipment · 56 Days

Capital Goods, Indian Tools

Industrialization becomes self-reinforcing when factories can increasingly purchase machines, controls and tooling from firms that share the same domestic engineering ecosystem. Capital goods are the machines that make other things, which means capability here spreads outward. Weakness here does the opposite: every expansion creates another import bill for sophisticated equipment and another external dependency for maintenance and upgrades.

The focus links the 2026 budget’s capital-goods measures to a longer effort around machine tools, industrial automation, testing systems and precision components. Public procurement becomes an early customer only when firms meet performance thresholds. Technical institutes partner with manufacturers around real equipment rather than generic curricula. Repair and retrofitting are treated as legitimate engineering industries instead of second-class substitutes for new purchases.

The result is not immediate technological independence. It is a thicker engineering base that learns from operating and improving machinery across sectors. A firm that masters a high-precision spindle for one application may become a supplier to another. A maintenance company can evolve into an equipment maker. Industrial knowledge begins to circulate laterally rather than arriving only inside imported boxes.

Firm Growth · 49 Days

The MSME Ladder

India has no shortage of small firms. The harder problem is enabling enough of them to become medium firms, then sophisticated suppliers, exporters and eventually large companies. Permanently small enterprises can provide livelihoods, but an economy in which firms avoid growth because taxation, credit, labour compliance or procurement becomes abruptly more difficult at a threshold is an economy that taxes ambition.

The MSME Ladder smooths those cliffs. Compliance obligations phase in more gradually, credit assessment uses better transaction data, cluster programmes support shared testing and training, and large public or private buyers are encouraged to publish supplier-development pathways rather than one-off local-content targets. Failure remains possible; the state does not guarantee every enterprise. It lowers the institutional penalty for trying to scale.

This produces one of the economic branch’s most socially significant effects. Productivity gains spread beyond a few national champions because smaller firms can join formal supply chains and pay for better equipment and skills. Workers gain more opportunities to move from informal, fragile employment into firms capable of investing in them. The ladder turns “small business support” from protection of size into support for progression.

Global Competitiveness · 56 Days

Export Discipline

Domestic scale can protect firms from the harshest forms of feedback. A manufacturer may survive with higher costs or variable quality because the home market is enormous. Export markets are less forgiving. They expose logistics delays, certification failures, weak design, unreliable suppliers and price disadvantages with brutal clarity. The focus uses that pressure deliberately.

Export support shifts away from chasing shipment volume alone. Standards laboratories, trade finance, customs predictability, logistics and market intelligence receive greater emphasis. Firms that repeatedly export receive incentives to invest in R&D and supplier development, while sectors dependent on permanent protection lose some advantages. The state still uses industrial policy, but it asks beneficiaries to demonstrate that capability can survive outside the domestic shelter.

The aim is not mercantilism. Imports remain essential for technology and competition, and the country does not try to maximize a trade surplus at any cost. Export discipline is a learning device: if Indian firms can win demanding customers elsewhere, they are more likely to become productive enough to raise wages and innovate at home. External markets become a benchmark for internal transformation.

Formalization · 49 Days

Formalization Without Exclusion

Formalization can expand tax capacity, worker protections, credit access and productivity, but it can also become a process by which the smallest firms discover that legal existence is expensive. Digital payments and tax systems have made economic activity more visible; the next challenge is ensuring that visibility creates a ladder into better institutions rather than a cliff that pushes activity back into informality.

The focus simplifies low-risk compliance, creates graduated obligations, expands portable social protection and reduces duplicative filings across levels of government. The state asks more of firms as they grow, but tries not to make one additional worker or one threshold of turnover trigger a disproportionate administrative shock. Formal status becomes valuable because it opens credit, procurement and insurance instead of merely creating liabilities.

The effect is slower than a mass registration campaign but more durable. Firms remain formal because participation improves access to markets and finance. Workers become more visible to social insurance systems. The tax base broadens without pretending every microenterprise should immediately behave like a listed company. The state gains information while the economy retains room for experimentation and entry.

Branch Capstone · 70 Days

The Productivity Republic

Productivity is the quiet variable beneath nearly every promise of prosperity. If a worker, machine, road, port, hectare, classroom or rupee of capital can produce more useful output, higher wages and better services become easier to sustain. If productivity stagnates, growth increasingly depends on adding more inputs, consuming more land and capital, or accepting thinner returns. The final economic focus therefore changes the campaign from accumulation to efficiency.

No ministry owns productivity. It emerges from the interaction of logistics, education, competition, city design, technology, capital goods, management quality and reliable institutions. This is why the capstone requires progress in exports, formalization and semiconductors. The player must demonstrate that India can connect infrastructure to firms, firms to technology, and technology to workers rather than treating each as a separate achievement.

The payoff compounds across the rest of the tree. Climate investment becomes cheaper per unit of resilience, public finance gains room because growth broadens the tax base, social programmes become easier to fund, and diplomatic autonomy increases because a more productive economy can absorb shocks. The Republic becomes wealthier not simply by doing more things, but by learning to do ordinary things with extraordinary consistency.
