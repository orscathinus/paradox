Event Bank

Twelve events establish the Version 1.0.0 event language. “Historical Anchor” means the premise is tied to a dated 2026 development; “Dynamic” means it is a plausible game event, not a claim that the event actually occurred.

IND.2026.001

The Monsoon Session

Dynamic · Politics
Union GovernmentCoalitionParliament

The parliamentary calendar compresses legislation, committee work, opposition scrutiny and coalition negotiation into a few weeks of visible national politics. Ministries arrive with bills they consider urgent; allies arrive with priorities that may have little to do with the ministries' original schedule.

For the government, the question is not whether bargaining occurs but whether it occurs early enough to avoid turning ordinary legislative management into a public crisis. The event models coalition arithmetic as an institution rather than a simple stability penalty.

“Consult first; move when the numbers are real.”Coalition Friction −5 · Political Power −15 · Stability +1%
“Keep the calendar moving.”Political Power +10 · 25% chance of Coalition Friction +8
IND.2026.002

The Second Semiconductor Push

Historical Anchor · Industry
July 2026Industrial PolicyTechnology

A larger second phase of India's semiconductor mission turns chips from an aspiration into a long-horizon industrial commitment. The headline funding is substantial, but officials inside the event know that fabs alone do not create an ecosystem.

Suppliers, packaging, engineering skills, design strength and patient quality control become the less glamorous variables. The player chooses whether to chase visible flagship capacity or spread resources across the industrial base that can make those facilities durable.

“Build the ecosystem beneath the fab.”Research +3% · Supplier Depth +8 · Build Speed −2%
“Prioritize visible capacity.”Industrial Prestige +8 · Construction Speed +5%
IND.2026.003

The Hormuz Premium

Dynamic · Energy
EnergyExternal ShockInflation

A new rise in Gulf risk pushes insurance, freight and crude prices upward. The geography is distant from New Delhi, but the consequences are domestic: transport costs, fertilizer, inflation expectations and the current account all begin to react.

The event gives the player no magic source of cheap energy. It asks which vulnerability to absorb now and which resilience to build for later—reserves, supplier diversity, efficiency or faster clean-energy investment.

“Use reserves to smooth the shock.”Inflation Pressure −4 · Strategic Reserves −8
“Protect the reserves; accelerate diversification.”Short-term Inflation +2 · Import Vulnerability −5
IND.2026.004

Ten Parachutes

Historical Anchor · Space
July 2026GaganyaanQualification

Another major test of the crew-module recovery system succeeds. The public story is a photograph and a short headline; inside the programme it is one more verified piece of a chain in which redundancy, sequencing and repeat testing matter more than drama.

The event reinforces the campaign's space philosophy. Human spaceflight prestige is earned by a system that can say “not ready” when evidence demands it, because every decision eventually carries people rather than only hardware.

“Test again before celebrating.”Space Safety +5 · Schedule Pressure +2
“Mark the milestone publicly.”National Prestige +4 · Space Programme Support +3
IND.2026.005

The Ally's Price

Dynamic · Coalition
Federal PoliticsBudgetRegional Priority

A coalition partner ties support for a national package to a long-requested project in its home state. Technically, the project can be justified. Politically, the timing makes the bargain obvious to everyone.

The dilemma is not corruption versus purity; it is whether negotiated regional priorities can be incorporated transparently into national planning without turning every vote into an auction. The event changes tone depending on whether the Coalition Dharma focus has been completed.

“Put it through the normal appraisal process.”Coalition Trust +2 · Delay project decision
“Make the package work.”Coalition Trust +5 · Fiscal Flexibility −3
IND.2026.006

A City Under Water

Dynamic · Urban
MonsoonMunicipal CapacityClimate

Hours of intense rain expose a familiar contradiction: expensive towers and new transit can coexist with drainage designed for another city and another climate. Roads disappear under water, commuters improvise routes home, and responsibility becomes fragmented across agencies.

The national government can fund emergency repair, but the event asks whether the deeper response changes planning incentives before memories fade. The best long-term option costs political capital because avoided floods never produce dramatic inauguration photographs.

“Fund metropolitan drainage reform.”Urban Capacity +5 · Treasury −20
“Repair what failed and restore service.”Short-term Stability +1% · Flood Risk unchanged
IND.2026.007

The Mountain Road Review

Dynamic · Infrastructure
Himalayan StatesInfrastructureResilience

A review of mountain infrastructure finds a familiar tradeoff between speed, environmental constraints, maintenance and the needs of remote communities. A road can serve national logistics and local development at the same time, but only if maintenance and slope stability are budgeted beyond construction day.

The event turns a strategic map line into a civil-engineering problem. The player can prioritize rapid completion or slower lifecycle planning that improves reliability after the opening ceremony.

“Design for the full lifecycle.”Infrastructure Reliability +6 · Build Speed −2%
“Finish the corridor first.”Build Speed +5% · Maintenance Cost +3%
IND.2026.008

The Finance Commission Era

Historical Anchor · Federalism
FY2026–27Fiscal FederalismStates

A new award period turns technical language about divisible pools and grants into practical questions of who pays, who delivers and who receives credit. States arrive with different demographic profiles, revenue capacity and development priorities.

The event does not offer a universally fair formula. It asks the player whether to reward need, performance, fiscal effort or stability—and reminds the player that any formula changes incentives as well as transfers money.

“Make the formula transparent and predictable.”State Cooperation +5 · Discretionary Funds −4
“Preserve room for targeted bargains.”Political Flexibility +5 · State Trust −2
IND.2026.009

Seven Fast Lines

Historical Anchor · Infrastructure
Union BudgetHigh-Speed RailUrban Systems

Seven proposed high-speed rail corridors look magnificent on a map. The planning files are less romantic: land, station access, feeder transit, operating models and the risk that speed between cities merely moves congestion to the station doors.

The event asks whether national prestige should follow top speed or network integration. A slower planning process can force housing and urban transit into the same conversation as the intercity line.

“Plan the station as part of the city.”Urban Integration +7 · Project Start Delay +90 days
“Protect the intercity schedule.”Rail Build Speed +5% · Urban Pressure +3
IND.2026.010

The Indian Ocean Table

Dynamic · Maritime
PortsRegional CooperationDisaster Response

Officials from several Indian Ocean states meet around an agenda dominated by shipping safety, disaster response, port continuity, weather information and undersea communications. None of the subjects produces a grand communiqué, but all of them reward habits of cooperation.

India can frame the initiative as a resident power offering public goods rather than demanding alignment. The event grants influence only if the player is willing to spend resources on capabilities that neighbours can actually use.

“Build the shared information network.”Regional Trust +6 · Maritime Awareness +4
“Keep commitments modest.”Treasury +10 · Regional Trust unchanged
IND.2026.011

The 2047 Review

Dynamic · Governance
Year-EndDevelopment LedgerMetrics

At year's end, ministries submit polished lists of schemes launched, money committed and projects inaugurated. The new development ledger asks a less comfortable question: what changed for travel time, learning, housing, power reliability, court delay and productive employment?

Several departments discover that activity is easier to count than outcomes. Publishing the scorecard may create criticism in the short term, but keeping it internal would weaken the entire logic of outcome-based administration.

“Publish it, including the weak indicators.”State Capacity +4 · Short-term Stability −1%
“Use it internally first.”Political Risk −2 · Reform Momentum −2
IND.2026.012

A Private Launch Economy

Dynamic · Space Industry
Commercial SpaceStartupsRegulation

India's commercial space sector begins to look less like a set of startups waiting around ISRO and more like an industry with its own customers, schedules and failure risks. That creates a new regulatory challenge: the state wants faster private growth without allowing one poorly governed accident to destroy confidence in the whole sector.

The event asks whether public infrastructure should be opened aggressively or expanded in stages while licensing and insurance systems mature. Both choices can grow the market; they distribute risk differently.

“Open access with strict, transparent rules.”Commercial Space +8 · Regulatory Workload +4
“Expand access in controlled phases.”Commercial Space +4 · Reliability +3
