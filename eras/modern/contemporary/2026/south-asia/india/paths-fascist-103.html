Version 1.0.3 · Fascist / Ultranationalist Route

The National State

This is the longest and most aggressive ideological route, but most of its length is spent constructing the regime that makes later expansion possible: centralization, political coercion, corporatist industry, youth mobilization, information control, demographic engineering and a militarized national culture. Conquest is the branch’s most visible endpoint, not its only content.

Fictional alternate-history framing: this route depicts an authoritarian fascist transformation and its costs. It is not presented as a historical inevitability or a recommendation. Expansion focuses stay at the strategic game level and include resistance, sanctions, repression and diplomatic consequences.

Mutually Exclusive Ideology Gate

⚑ The National State

Locks Democratic / Communist / Imperial routesAuthoritarian Mobilization

The fascist route begins with a rejection of the Republic’s central bargain: difference will no longer be negotiated indefinitely. The movement argues that federal vetoes, party competition and social pluralism have prevented India from acting with the speed its scale demands. A new executive order is promised in their place.

Power concentrates quickly, but concentration is not the same as capacity. Ministries discover that officials still need information from states, firms still need suppliers, and a decree cannot make a train arrive on time. The regime responds by turning political loyalty into a parallel administrative structure rather than admitting that complexity survives ideology.

India becomes easier to command at the top and harder to hear from below. The player gains speed, mobilization and coercive tools while losing the adaptive feedback of an open political system. Every later focus is built on that exchange.

Gameplay: political action speed +; dissent and rights penalties; unlocks corporatist, mobilization and territorial-revision branches.
Centralization

⚔ One State, One Will

The Union begins stripping away the ambiguity that once defined Indian federalism. Governors, centrally appointed administrators and emergency legal instruments become routine tools for forcing state governments into line.

At first the system looks efficient. National projects stop waiting for protracted bargaining and unpopular measures move quickly. Then the hidden costs appear: local officials learn to report what Delhi wants to hear, regional parties become permanent opposition movements, and implementation errors spread farther before anyone feels safe enough to describe them.

The state has fewer veto points and fewer warning lights. That makes it powerful in a crisis and brittle in a long campaign. The focus deliberately gives the player both sides of the bargain.

Gameplay: central authority +; state compliance through coercion +; intelligence quality and federal legitimacy −.
Political Economy

🏢 The Corporate State

Rather than abolish private capital, the regime seeks to discipline it. Industrial associations, state officials and approved labor bodies are organized into sectoral councils that decide production priorities, investment targets and wage frameworks.

Large firms gain access to state contracts and protection in exchange for obedience. Independent unions lose bargaining power, while favored industrial houses become deeply entangled with the regime. The boundary between public policy and political patronage grows deliberately thin.

The system can mobilize astonishing resources for strategic sectors, but innovation outside official priorities suffers. The economy begins to resemble the political order: fast when everyone is pointed in one direction, clumsy when the future requires an unexpected turn.

Gameplay: strategic-industry build speed +; independent labor support −; corruption/patronage risk +.
Domestic Regime

⚙ The National Labor Charter

The regime cannot build a mass politics on industrialists alone. It therefore abolishes independent class conflict in theory and replaces it with a state-mediated “national labor community.” Workers are promised housing, pensions and predictable employment if they accept limits on strikes and autonomous organization.

Factory disputes become administrative cases rather than political struggles. In prosperous sectors the bargain can feel materially real; in neglected ones, compulsory harmony looks more like silence imposed from above.

The labor charter gives fascist India a social base distinct from simple oligarchy. It also creates a vast promise the state must finance. A regime that claims every worker as part of the national project becomes responsible for their welfare in ways a less ambitious state could evade.

Gameplay: industrial stability +; strike risk −; welfare cost +; civil liberties −.
Mass Politics

🚩 Youth of the Nation

Schools, universities and youth organizations become recruiting grounds for a politics of service, discipline and national mission. Uniformed volunteer corps appear at public events, infrastructure drives and disaster exercises.

The movement is careful at first to frame participation as civic duty rather than paramilitary preparation. Over time the distinction erodes. Advancement in public institutions increasingly rewards visible participation, and social pressure turns “voluntary” service into an expectation.

The regime gains a generation trained to identify personal ambition with state ambition. It also narrows the space in which young Indians can imagine a future outside the official national project.

Gameplay: mobilization and manpower +; youth dissent − through coercion; research creativity slight penalty.
Information

📡 Command the Narrative

A continental country cannot be mobilized if every television studio, newspaper, filmmaker and online platform tells a different story. The regime therefore builds licensing, ownership and regulatory systems that reward alignment and punish persistent opposition.

The propaganda apparatus is modern rather than merely censorious. Data analytics, influencer networks, entertainment programming and patriotic spectacle are used alongside formal restrictions. The state learns that people absorb a worldview more easily through stories than through decrees.

Short-term cohesion improves. Long-term information quality degrades. Bad news now travels upward slowly because officials fear the political meaning attached to admitting failure, forcing the regime to spend more on intelligence simply to understand the country it rules.

Gameplay: stability and mobilization propaganda +; intelligence reliability and innovation feedback −.
Identity

🜂 Cultural Unification

The fascist movement argues that India’s diversity is strategically expensive. National curricula, ceremonies and language policy are rewritten around a narrower civilizational narrative meant to produce a more uniform political identity.

Some citizens embrace the clarity. Others experience the new program as a demand that regional, religious or linguistic identities shrink to fit an official hierarchy. The state answers resistance with a mixture of incentives and coercion.

The result is a national culture that looks more synchronized from Delhi and more contested in the regions. The player gains ideological conformity at the price of long-term resistance in places where belonging once depended on federal pluralism.

Gameplay: core-state ideological support +; minority-region resistance +; multilingual administration penalties.
Symbolic Power

🏛 A Capital for a Great Power

Authoritarian regimes understand architecture as an argument made in stone. New Delhi becomes a showcase of monumental avenues, ministries, memorials and public spaces designed to make the regime appear permanent.

The projects generate construction demand and visual spectacle, but their opportunity cost is impossible to hide. Municipal systems elsewhere ask why drainage, housing and local transit compete with prestige buildings for the same fiscal room.

The focus gives the fascist route something other than conquest: a politics of visible grandeur. It increases prestige and regime identity while reminding the player that symbolism is expensive precisely because it produces no direct strategic resource.

Gameplay: prestige and stability +; construction consumption +; urban service investment opportunity cost.
Social Engineering

👥 Demographic Discipline

The regime begins treating population not as citizens with independent choices but as a strategic resource. Family policy, migration incentives, workforce participation and settlement priorities are folded into national planning.

Some measures are familiar—childcare, housing incentives, rural-to-urban planning—but the authoritarian frame changes their meaning. Benefits become entangled with political expectations, and demographic statistics acquire the cold vocabulary of national strength.

The branch can improve labor mobilization and settlement planning, but it also increases coercion and resentment. The player is forced to see how quickly a state that views people as inputs begins redesigning private life around political objectives.

Gameplay: manpower and labor-force planning +; rights and happiness −; migration control +.
Militarized State

🛡 The Martial Republic

The regime normalizes permanent preparedness. Civil defence, reserve training, logistics exercises and patriotic service become regular features of national life long before an actual war begins.

Factories are mapped for conversion, rail timetables are tested against mobilization plans, and state administrations receive security targets alongside development goals. The distinction between civilian efficiency and military readiness begins to blur.

This does not yet require conquest. It creates the institutional machinery that makes conquest plausible later. India becomes harder to surprise and more willing to interpret ordinary policy through the lens of strategic competition.

Gameplay: mobilization speed, logistics reserve and war readiness +; civilian consumption and diplomatic trust −.
Industrial Mobilization

🏭 The Autarkic Imperative

The regime views dependence as vulnerability. Energy equipment, machine tools, electronics, chemicals and transport systems are screened for foreign chokepoints, and domestic substitutes receive aggressive state support.

Autarky sounds simple until engineers list the thousands of specialized components buried inside modern industry. The program therefore becomes less about literal self-sufficiency than about reducing the number of external suppliers who can halt strategic production.

Costs rise and some technology arrives more slowly. In exchange, the state gains a thicker domestic industrial base that can survive sanctions better than an economy optimized purely for price.

Gameplay: sanctions resilience +; import efficiency −; strategic supplier depth +.
Technology

✈ Arms, Engines and Aerospace

Military prestige shifts from buying impressive systems to mastering the industries beneath them. Engines, sensors, materials, avionics and production tooling receive the kind of long-horizon investment usually lost behind headline procurement.

The regime markets these programs as proof of national virility, but the engineers know slogans cannot shorten metallurgical learning curves. Testing failures, redesigns and supplier quality become uncomfortable but unavoidable parts of the effort.

By the end of the focus, India has not magically solved every technology gap. It has built more of the institutional ecosystem needed to keep trying without restarting from zero each time.

Gameplay: aerospace and military-industry research +; high fiscal cost; civilian R&D crowding risk.
Expansion Doctrine

🗺 The Subcontinent Doctrine

Only after the regime has centralized politics, disciplined labor and prepared industry does it formally redefine India’s regional aims. South Asia is no longer described as a neighbourhood of sovereign equals but as a strategic space in which Delhi claims primacy.

The doctrine does not immediately specify borders. It establishes a hierarchy: small states are expected to align, larger rivals are expected to accept limits, and Indian security concerns are presumed to outweigh competing regional claims.

This is the hinge between domestic fascism and territorial revision. From here the tree becomes more aggressive, but the same doctrine can also produce protectorates, coerced economic blocs and military access without immediate annexation.

Gameplay: unlocks coercive diplomacy and war-goal branches; world tension +; neighbour trust collapses.
Maldives

🏝 The Malé Ultimatum

The regime identifies the Maldives as the test of whether the Subcontinent Doctrine means anything. Malé is presented with demands for basing access, foreign-policy alignment and restrictions on outside military relationships.

From Delhi’s perspective the islands are tiny points on a strategic map. From Malé’s perspective they are a sovereign country being told that geography has cancelled independence. International attention arrives quickly.

The player can stop at coerced alignment or proceed toward annexation. Even the “successful” ultimatum carries costs: fear spreads through every other small state in the Indian Ocean.

Gameplay: demands strategic subordination; may yield access without war; diplomatic isolation and regional fear +.
Territorial Expansion

⚓ Annex the Maldives

The most direct island-expansion option discards even the fiction of partnership. India claims that permanent control of the archipelago is necessary for maritime security and moves to absorb it into the national system.

Strategically, the islands offer valuable position. Politically, annexation transforms them from geographic assets into a population that must be governed, supplied and persuaded—or coerced—to accept a state they did not choose.

The game deliberately refuses to make this free territory. Resistance, sanctions, diplomatic condemnation and long-distance administrative costs follow immediately. Expansion changes the map and creates a new problem that remains after the map animation ends.

Gameplay: annexation/war-goal path; maritime position +; resistance, sanctions and occupation cost sharply +.
Pakistan

☄ The Western Reckoning

The regime reinterprets the India–Pakistan rivalry as an unfinished territorial settlement rather than a durable interstate reality. Historical grievance becomes official strategic doctrine.

Mobilization language intensifies, trade links shrink and diplomatic space narrows. The government argues that only decisive pressure can end decades of insecurity, while military planners confront the enormous risks hidden inside that phrase.

The focus is intentionally a warning light as much as a power-up. It opens the route to direct confrontation but also makes crisis management more difficult and increases the chance that a smaller incident spirals beyond the player’s control.

Gameplay: Pakistan claims/pressure options; war support +; crisis escalation and international alarm sharply +.
Territorial Expansion · Extreme Risk

⚔ Subdue Pakistan

The regime commits to breaking Pakistan as an independent strategic rival. In game terms this is the fascist branch’s most consequential territorial focus, not a routine extension of the border.

Even victory would create a political problem of extraordinary scale: a large population, deep national identity, complex geography and powerful external reactions. Occupation cannot be represented honestly as a few green map tiles.

The focus therefore delivers immense potential power and immense permanent burden. A player who chooses it is choosing decades of resistance, diplomatic isolation and resource diversion alongside any territorial gain.

Gameplay: major war-goal/puppet/partition options; massive resistance, occupation and sanctions mechanics; world tension surge.
Bangladesh

🌾 The Eastern Question

Bangladesh sits at the mouth of rivers and transport corridors that shape eastern India. The fascist government increasingly portrays Dhaka’s independent policy as an intolerable strategic uncertainty.

Pressure begins through trade, transit and security demands rather than immediate invasion. The northeast’s access to the sea becomes a recurring argument for a more subordinate Bangladesh.

The branch can end in a coerced alliance, protectorate arrangement or direct domination. Each option trades different amounts of international cost for different degrees of control.

Gameplay: coercive leverage over Bangladesh; unlocks protectorate or conquest branch; trade friction +.
Bangladesh · Expansion

⚑ Dominate Bangladesh

The regime decides that influence is insufficient. Bangladesh is to be integrated into an Indian-led regional order under conditions defined in Delhi.

Direct annexation is only one possible outcome. A dependent regime, military treaty or territorial restructuring can satisfy the focus depending on earlier decisions and wartime results.

Whatever the form, the political cost is high. Bengali identity does not disappear because the strategic map says the delta would be convenient to control. The branch carries long-term resistance and legitimacy penalties if coercion replaces local consent.

Gameplay: puppet/annexation options; eastern logistics +; resistance and diplomatic penalties scale with coercion.
Nepal

🏔 Cast the Himalayan Shadow

Nepal’s location becomes intolerable to a regime obsessed with strategic depth. Delhi demands that Kathmandu limit outside security relationships and coordinate border, infrastructure and foreign policy with India.

The goal is initially subordination, not annexation. A compliant Nepal can serve as buffer, market and mountain partner without the cost of direct administration.

But the very pressure meant to secure the Himalaya can drive Nepali nationalism in the opposite direction, making China more attractive as a counterweight. The focus creates leverage and risk at the same time.

Gameplay: protectorate demands; India–China rivalry interaction; Nepal resistance rises if pressure is excessive.
Nepal · Expansion

♜ Subjugate Nepal

If Kathmandu refuses alignment, the fascist branch can abandon the buffer-state model and seek direct control. The Himalaya becomes an imperial frontier even though the regime rejects imperial terminology.

Mountain geography makes governance expensive and military access difficult. The symbolic cost is larger still: India’s claim to be liberating the region from outside influence collapses when it eliminates a neighbour’s sovereignty.

The focus grants strategic depth while creating a permanent legitimacy problem. It is a classic grand-strategy bargain: the border looks cleaner; the political map becomes more combustible.

Gameplay: Nepal war-goal/puppet path; mountain access +; resistance, supply and China-tension costs +.
Sri Lanka

🌊 The Ceylon Outpost

Sri Lanka occupies too important a position for the regime to leave its ports entirely outside Indian strategic planning. Delhi seeks permanent access arrangements and political guarantees.

The initial offer is transactional: infrastructure, trade and security support in exchange for naval privileges. If Colombo resists, the branch can become coercive without automatically requiring annexation.

This is one of the focuses that shows fascist expansion can mean hierarchy rather than constant border changes. India’s map may remain the same while its strategic footprint grows far beyond it.

Gameplay: basing and alignment options in Sri Lanka; maritime reach +; regional fear +.
China

🐉 Challenge China for Asia

After imposing a regional hierarchy closer to home, the regime turns north and east. China is no longer merely a border rival; it is the competing claim to continental primacy.

India expands mountain infrastructure, arms production, partnerships with states wary of Beijing and maritime pressure in the Indian Ocean. Propaganda frames the rivalry as a civilizational contest, making compromise politically harder even when strategy might benefit from it.

The focus opens both containment and direct confrontation. It grants prestige and mobilization but creates the dangerous possibility that the regime becomes trapped by the maximalist language it used to build support.

Gameplay: China rivalry +++; border and maritime preparedness +; diplomatic flexibility −; war path unlocked but not mandatory.
Late-Game Mobilization

🏭 The Continental War Economy

Expansion turns the industrial state toward sustained conflict. Civilian production is reorganized around transport, fuel, munitions, spare parts and strategic stockpiles.

The transformation reveals why the earlier domestic focuses mattered. A regime that had only demanded patriotic enthusiasm would now fail; one that built supplier depth, logistics reserves and a disciplined industrial bureaucracy can sustain pressure longer.

The price is borne in living standards and consumer choice. The state may win strategic endurance while hollowing out the prosperity it claimed expansion would secure.

Gameplay: war production and logistics +++; consumer economy −; fatigue and rationing mechanics intensify.
Fascist Capstone

⚑ The Greater Indian Order

The fascist endgame is not a single border. It is a hierarchy. Protectorates, annexed territories, coerced allies and strategic bases are organized into an Indian-dominated order extending across much of South Asia and the Indian Ocean.

The regime celebrates the map as proof that discipline overcame the compromises of the old Republic. Beneath the ceremonies sits a more complicated reality: occupation budgets, resistant populations, nervous client governments and rivals looking for opportunities to break the system apart.

The branch ends at maximum visible power and maximum political load. India has enlarged the space it controls, but every additional territory has also enlarged the number of people who must accept—or resist—the national state.

Gameplay: regional-hegemony capstone; large resource/manpower potential; permanent resistance, sanctions and overextension pressure.
