DIPLOMACY & WORLD ROLE · 70 DAYS

◉ Guard the Energy Lifelines

India's growth remains exposed to energy prices formed far beyond the country's borders. A disruption in the Gulf can move rapidly through shipping costs, transport, fertilizer, industrial inputs, inflation and household budgets. The campaign therefore treats energy security as a domestic economic question as much as a diplomatic one.

The focus builds resilience through several imperfect tools: more diverse suppliers and contract structures, larger buffers, flexible refining, more efficient freight and faster deployment of electricity sources that reduce imported-fuel exposure over time. None removes risk on its own. The strategy is to make external shocks arrive through more than one channel so that no single disruption dominates the whole system.

The broader lesson is material. Strategic autonomy means less when ordinary economic life can be sharply taxed by a distant chokepoint. Every improvement in efficiency, storage, grid strength and supplier diversity expands the range of choices available to policymakers during a crisis. In this branch, sovereignty is measured partly in how many shocks the economy can absorb without abandoning its long-term plans.
