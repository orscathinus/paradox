⚙ Industrial Resilience

India's strategic industries sit inside global supply chains rather than outside them. Components, machine tools, software, specialised materials and maintenance knowledge often cross borders many times before a finished system is usable. The scenario therefore treats self-reliance as a question of resilience and learning rather than a demand that every input carry a domestic label.

This focus rewards programmes that build reliable local suppliers, testing capacity, repair skills, engineering depth and predictable long-term procurement. Foreign partnerships can still be valuable when they transfer know-how and create durable supplier relationships. Domestic production can still fail when it becomes protected from demanding standards. The player is asked to balance continuity of supply with the discipline that comes from competition and technical scrutiny.

The flavor is about endurance. A country gains strategic room when critical industries can maintain what they build, substitute around disrupted inputs and improve designs over time. That requires patient institutions more than slogans: purchasing rules that value lifecycle support, technical education linked to real factories, and firms that survive long enough to accumulate tacit knowledge.
