▦ Semicon 2.0

The chip is tiny; the ecosystem required to make it is not.

India's 2026 semiconductor programme enters a larger second phase at a moment when chips have become central to industrial planning worldwide. A strategy game can make this look deceptively simple: fund a factory and gain technology. Real semiconductor manufacturing depends on an unusually dense web of design tools, equipment, gases, chemicals, packaging, stable utilities, specialised maintenance and experienced workers.

India brings substantial chip-design and software talent to this challenge, together with a large domestic electronics market. The focus therefore looks beyond headline fabrication plants. It gives equal weight to advanced packaging, compound semiconductors, supplier development, equipment servicing, university fabrication access and workforce training. The goal in the scenario is not to manufacture every input domestically, but to deepen the parts of the value chain where repeated production can create durable capability.

The shift also changes the country's industrial identity. Software and services remain major strengths, but a deeper semiconductor ecosystem would tie digital expertise to precision physical production. The strategic test is whether institutions can coordinate patient learning across firms, universities and public programmes long enough for the ecosystem to become more than a collection of subsidised projects.
