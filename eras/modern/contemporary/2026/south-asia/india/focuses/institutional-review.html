🛡 Institutional Review

A state becomes more capable when important programmes can be examined without turning every review into a contest over prestige. The starting point of this focus is therefore administrative: responsibilities are mapped clearly, evaluation criteria are written before projects are judged, and records are kept in forms that can survive changes of leadership.

Different organisations will continue to disagree, and the scenario does not pretend that coordination makes hard choices disappear. Instead, it gives disagreements a process. Findings move between offices, recurring maintenance and staffing problems are visible earlier, and programme managers have to explain what they learned from delays or failures rather than simply asking for another cycle of funding.

The flavor is intentionally unglamorous. Institutional memory, audit trails and routine review do not produce parade-ground imagery, yet they determine whether a large public system improves from one year to the next. In game terms, this focus rewards the connective tissue of administration rather than any specific operational capability.
