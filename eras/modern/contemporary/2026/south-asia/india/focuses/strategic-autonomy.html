DIPLOMACY & WORLD ROLE · 35 DAYS

🌐 Strategic Autonomy, Updated

India's foreign policy has long placed unusual value on freedom of decision. The habit reflects history, geography and scale: a very large country with varied interests is reluctant to let one partnership determine every other relationship. By the 2020s this is often described as strategic autonomy rather than simple non-alignment, because India is deeply cooperative with multiple powers even while preserving independent choices.

The difficulty in 2026 is that international issues increasingly overlap. Technology rules affect industry, energy markets affect diplomacy, and supply chains acquire geopolitical meaning. The focus therefore represents autonomy as capability rather than distance. India can cooperate closely in one domain, disagree in another, and evaluate each arrangement by what it adds to resilience, market access, technology, influence or room for manoeuvre.

This is not a doctrine of equidistance. Interests are not symmetrical, and relationships need not be equally close. The campaign rewards a diplomacy that remains self-directed while recognising that autonomy without domestic capacity can become rhetorical. Options are valuable only when the country possesses enough economic and institutional depth to exercise them.
