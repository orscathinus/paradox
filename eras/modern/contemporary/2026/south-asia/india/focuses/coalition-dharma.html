🏛 Coalition Dharma

A focus on coalition management as an institutional mechanic.

India enters 2026 with a coalition government at the Union level. That changes the texture of decision-making even when the prime minister and the broad direction of government remain continuous. Allies have formal leverage, regional priorities receive more attention inside national bargaining, and parliamentary management becomes more visible than it was during periods when a single party held a majority on its own.

Coalition politics in a federal country is rarely only about personalities. Infrastructure allocations, fiscal transfers, language, welfare design, agricultural interests and state-specific development priorities all enter negotiation. A policy that appears national from New Delhi may create sharply different incentives in Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu or Maharashtra. The mechanical question for this campaign is therefore not whether bargaining exists, but whether it is predictable enough for the state to keep executing long-term programmes.

This focus establishes routine consultation with coalition partners and stronger coordination before major legislation or budget commitments. It deliberately imposes a small cost to speed in exchange for fewer surprise breakdowns later. In gameplay terms, coalition becomes a system to manage rather than a temporary penalty to remove; in lore terms, it reflects one of the recurring ways a highly diverse parliamentary federation turns disagreement into government.
