🪷 Renew the Federal Compact

National scale only works when state-level government can adapt it.

India is one Union, but it is not one administration. State governments run enormous portions of public life, and several Indian states contain populations comparable to major countries. Fiscal transfers, centrally sponsored schemes, tax devolution and grants therefore shape political reality as surely as debates in Parliament. The 2026–27 fiscal cycle also begins the period associated with the Sixteenth Finance Commission, keeping the distribution of resources between the Union and the states near the centre of institutional politics.

A renewed compact treats the states less as implementation arms and more as governing partners. New Delhi can define national objectives while allowing meaningful variation in delivery, publish common outcome standards, and make interstate forums more useful for problems such as migration, logistics, water, electricity and urban corridors that do not respect administrative boundaries. Competition among states remains; the objective is to make competition reward performance rather than reward only access to the centre.

The deeper wager is constitutional as much as administrative. A confident Union does not need to make every operational choice itself. The Republic's size becomes manageable when national scale is combined with state-level experimentation, local information and a fiscal system that makes responsibilities intelligible. Federalism then ceases to appear only as friction and becomes one of the mechanisms by which a continent-sized democracy can move at all.
